# QuotePlate: one restaurant, one real purchase

Prepared 11 September 2026. **Kit ready; real trial NOT PERFORMED.** No participants have been supplied, invitations sent, purchases made or results measured for this kit.

Use one restaurant, its owner/buyer, one employee who normally handles purchases (the owner may fill both roles), and **3–5 existing suppliers**. Use their usual phones and connection. The restaurant chooses a normal, small purchase and remains responsible for sending messages, selecting suppliers and confirming the order.

## Before the restaurant starts

- Check that the hosted site loads and **Get started** opens Google sign-in before arranging the session. Measure the restaurant's actual signup during the trial; pause if loading or signup prevents progress.
- Fill in: restaurant code ___; buyer code ___; employee code ___; supplier codes ___; trial date ___; quote deadline including IST ___; delivery date ___. Use codes in shared observations; keep identities and contact details with the restaurant.
- Ask the restaurant to choose 3–5 willing existing suppliers, at least two able to quote the same three items. Use about five needed items with clear names, specifications, quantities and units. If that overlap is impossible, record it; a comparable-offer test will remain incomplete.
- Bring the actual shopping list/photo and, at delivery, the actual invoice. Ask suppliers who already have a printed price list to have it ready. Do not create a cleaner substitute and label it real-world evidence. Obtain agreement to observe and record task timings; a participant can stop.
- Use existing devices, WhatsApp and the current hosted service. Buy no messaging, OCR, search or other paid service. Normal ingredient spending stays with the restaurant. Staff time, data and hosting usage are not automatically zero cost; record unknown costs as unknown.

## Short instructions for the restaurant

1. **Start your workspace.** Open QuotePlate, choose **Get started**, enter your restaurant details and continue with Google. A new customer should use their own verified Google account. If signup or loading blocks you, tell the observer and pause.
2. **Save your supplier contacts first.** Under **Suppliers → Add existing contacts**, paste one supplier per line: business name, phone, optional email, without column headings. Choose **Review contacts**, check each number belongs to the intended business, then press **Add 3 suppliers** (the count follows your list). Wait for confirmation that all 3–5 contacts are saved. If they are already saved, check those records instead of adding duplicates. Adding contacts does not send invitations. Finish this before starting a new purchase so you do not leave an unsaved draft.
3. **Make and save the shopping list.** Open **New purchase** (or **Purchases → Choose ingredients**). Enter a **Request title**. Choose **Add a shopping list**; type your five items and press **Review text**, or choose a printed-English photo, press **Read photo**, check the recognised text and then press **Review text**. Check every name, quantity and unit against your original, select the intended rows and press **Add checked rows to draft**. No menu is needed. Under **Ask which suppliers?**, select your saved suppliers. Complete **Delivery and timing**, including address, city, state, PIN, delivery date and **Quote deadline (India time)** before delivery. Include needed specifications in the item names and any common requirements under **Payment and order terms (optional)**. Press **Save draft** and wait for the saved purchase page before navigating away.
4. **Open the saved purchase for prices.** Review its items, suppliers and deadlines. If needed, use **Edit draft**, then **Save changes**. Press **Create supplier links** and confirm “Open this request and create one private quote link for each supplier?” Wait for the request-opened confirmation and the private supplier links below. This opens quoting; it does not send messages.
5. **Send each supplier their own link.** For each supplier's link, choose **Share on WhatsApp**, check the recipient and message, then **you press Send** in WhatsApp. If sharing fails, use **Copy link**, paste it into that supplier's chat and send. Save/share the link before leaving its creation screen; a lost link must be replaced. Never put private links in the shared observation CSV.
6. **Review the quotes at the agreed deadline.** Check item specification, pack/unit, quantity, GST, freight, minimum order, availability, delivery date and payment terms. Compare the same basket; missing terms mean an incomplete offer. The owner selects the supplier(s) and records the award. The restaurant confirms the order through its usual channel; QuotePlate does not automatically message the supplier. If the purchase cannot wait, use the normal process and record the QuotePlate step as incomplete.
7. **Check delivery and invoice.** Count what arrived and what you reject. In the delivery check, choose **Read invoice photo or text** if useful. Compare each suggested billed quantity and unit rate with the invoice before applying it. Matching needs the exact awarded item name and unit. Enter physical received/rejected quantities separately, check invoice total/tax/freight yourself and explicitly save the delivery check. Leave partial deliveries and unresolved credits open; do not mark them settled without evidence.

The invoice helper does not infer receipts, tax, totals, credits or payments. Manual entries remain available when a photo is unclear.

## Short instructions for suppliers

1. Open your private quote link on your phone. **No QuotePlate account, Google account, email account or app installation is needed.**
2. Enter prices and available quantities for the requested items. Check units; mark unavailable items and explain substitutions. Fill in GST, freight, delivery and payment terms.
3. If you already have a printed price list, try **Use a price list**. Review the read text, item matches, units and rates, then choose **Use checked prices**. Existing manual entries stay in place. Type prices instead if the photo does not work.
4. Review the quote and press Submit. Tell the observer when you finish. A WhatsApp reply alone does not appear in QuotePlate's comparison.

Keep first-quote work to ten minutes. If stuck, ask for help or return to your usual quoting method; the observer records that outcome. Supplier business-profile setup and separate workspace links are optional and are not prerequisites for this quote.

## Observer: one small sheet, three questions

Use [restaurant-first-purchase-observations.csv](restaurant-first-purchase-observations.csv). It intentionally contains only column headings; no placeholder people or results are recorded. Give a neutral two-minute introduction, then let people try. Record requests, hints, translation/read-aloud help and takeovers; any of these makes completion assisted. After a two-minute stall, offer one neutral hint and note it. Do not silently complete the task for them.

**1. Can this restaurant reach its suppliers?** Before inviting, freeze the 3–5 supplier codes as the sample. Check the restaurant's existing contacts. For each supplier, record `known_contact` as yes/no/unknown after the restaurant checks identity and the usable channel. Count confirmed existing contacts / all sampled suppliers. This measures the restaurant's address book, not discovery quality.

Separately, spend at most ten minutes checking public contact coverage for this same sample. Where a supplier has a known public website, try **Find contacts on the supplier's website**; compare suggestions with the restaurant's known contact and the cited source. Record `website_available`, `website_attempted`, `website_contact_correct` and `website_contact_wrong`; record blocked pages, missing contacts or no website explicitly. Report correct usable website contacts / all sampled suppliers **and** / suppliers whose websites were actually checked. Do not drop failures from the second denominator. Zero eligible websites means website extraction is **not tested**, not successful. A published phone/email alone does not prove WhatsApp reachability or delivery coverage. This convenience sample cannot estimate India-wide coverage.

For each invitation, record `sent_at`, `opened_at` if observed, and `submitted_at`. The restaurant's Send action is the sending evidence. QuotePlate views and quote submissions are not WhatsApp delivery/read receipts. Record wrong numbers, no response by deadline, handler failure, missing browser access and manual-copy fallback separately.

**2. Do real photos save effort after review?** Observe the real shopping-list photo, actual invoice photo and each willing supplier's existing price-list photo. Record document type, printed/handwritten/mixed language, phone/browser/network and image-quality issue. Supported photo use is printed English, within the documented 8 MB/dimension limits; try manual/text entry when unsuitable. If only handwriting, a PDF or another language is available, record that real-world format gap. Do not claim supported OCR accuracy from it.

For each attempted photo, compare every relevant source row with the first suggestions and with the final saved/submitted values. Count `source_rows`, `raw_correct_rows`, `missed_rows`, `extra_rows`, `corrected_rows` and `final_error_rows`. A row is raw-correct only if every needed field is right: item/quantity/unit for a shopping list, item/unit/rate for a price list, and matched awarded item/unit/billed quantity/rate for an invoice. Missing or wrongly matched rows are not correct. Record recognition wait and review/correction time separately. Report raw-correct rows / source rows for each document type, plus extras separately; no relevant rows means not tested. Final values must be checked even when recognition looks accurate.

**3. Can vendors finish on their own phones?** For every invited supplier, record the outcome at the deadline, including nonresponders. Time first link opening to correct submission, and separately Send to submission. Note the first confusing step, help, phone/network constraints and whether they would use it for the next purchase. A quote completed by the observer is assisted. An account/login demand on the supplier link is a blocker. Two short debrief questions: “What slowed you down?” and “Would you use this for your next purchase? Why?”

## Decision criteria — targets, not results

Apply these targets to the first run; they are practical pilot thresholds, not market benchmarks. Timing includes loading and correction. Record all failed/incomplete tasks even if the restaurant finishes the purchase elsewhere.

| Check | Target for this trial |
| --- | --- |
| Start | Actual restaurant signup reaches a usable workspace within 5 minutes without operator takeover. Record any loading or signup block. |
| Restaurant draft | Correct five-item purchase saved with selected suppliers and deadlines within 10 minutes of starting list entry, with no help beyond the introduction. Setup/contact checking and creating/sharing links are timed separately. |
| Contact coverage | All 3–5 intended recipients have restaurant-confirmed usable contacts before sending. Public website coverage is reported as counts with both denominators; no minimum discovery coverage is assumed. |
| Vendor completion | At least 80% of all invited suppliers submit correct quotes unaided within 10 minutes of their first opening and before the agreed deadline: **3/3, 4/4 or 4/5**. Nonresponders remain in the invited denominator. |
| Useful comparison | At least two current offers cover the same three or more items with all comparison terms confirmed. Owner reviews and records the award within 10 minutes of starting comparison. Missing overlap/terms means this check is incomplete. |
| Photo usability | For each supported photo attempted: at least 80% raw-correct source rows, no final errors, and review/correction completed within 5 minutes after recognition. Report recognition time separately. A missing real photo leaves that document type untested; manual fallback can complete the purchase but cannot pass its photo check. |
| Invoice and receiving | Employee correctly saves the actual delivery/invoice check within 15 minutes, with no takeover. All billed values agree with the invoice, physical counts are independently checked, and any real discrepancy/credit remains accurately recorded. No invented delivery exceptions. |
| Critical errors | Zero unresolved wrong recipients, exposed private records, wrong units/quantities/rates/totals or wrong awards. Stop the affected step if observed; correct before buying and retain the incident in the report. |

**Decision:** recommend a second purchase only when the core start, draft, contact, vendor, comparison, receiving and critical-error checks meet their targets. Failed photo checks mean use manual entry and fix/retest the helper; untested photo types remain explicit evidence gaps. A second purchase is a later restaurant decision, not an automated follow-up. One successful purchase does not establish savings, broad adoption or competitor superiority.

## CSV recording and closeout

Keep one row per measurement; give related rows the same `task_id`. Use participant codes only. `stage` can be `readiness`, `signup`, `draft`, `contact`, `share`, `quote`, `compare`, `ocr_list`, `ocr_price`, `ocr_invoice`, `receiving` or `debrief`. Record one `measure` and `value` with `unit` (count, seconds, yes/no, text or timestamp); use the measure names above for contacts and photos. Save timestamps with `+05:30` so elapsed quote turnaround is calculable.

For each attempted task, add one row with measure `task_summary`. Fill `outcome` with `unaided`, `assisted`, `incomplete`, `abandoned` or `not_tested`; give a reason for the last three. Only this summary row carries `help_count`, `active_seconds` and `wait_seconds`, preventing double-counted time. Total task time is active plus waiting, from instruction/opening to correct saved result or stop. For OCR, add `recognition_seconds` and `review_seconds` measurements. Record nonresponders as incomplete at deadline without inventing active work time. Use blank numeric cells when unknown, never zero by assumption. Include help type and final/critical error counts in `notes`, plus device/browser/network and English/typing comfort on the first participant row. `evidence_ref` holds a private local reference or nonsensitive source URL; exclude private access links, contact details and invoice images from committed observations.

At closeout, write a short result note: deployed revision/date; actual participant counts; each criterion met/missed/not tested; contact ratios; each photo's row counts and times; supplier outcomes out of all invited; median/range of completed task times and separate timeouts; help and critical incidents; unresolved delivery/credits; top three fixes; willingness to repeat. Record usual-method times only if actually observed, label recollections as estimates, and do not claim savings from them. Compare prices only on the same current basket and complete terms.
